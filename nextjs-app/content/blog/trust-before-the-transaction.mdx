Coinbase just gave AI agents the ability to trade and pay autonomously.

That's a real unlock. It's also the moment the trust question stops being theoretical.
An agent that can move money needs to know who it's transacting with — *before* the
transaction clears, not after it's been disputed.

## The verification layer

SAID provides that check. For any counterparty, an agent can read:

- **Identity status** — is this agent registered and verified, or anonymous?
- **Trust tier** — where does it sit relative to the rest of the network?
- **Reputation score** — derived from real on-chain interactions, not self-reported claims

It's the same question every human payment rail answered decades ago — *who am I paying?* —
applied to a world where the parties are autonomous software settling value at machine speed.

## Why now

Autonomous payments without a trust layer just relocate the risk. As more agents gain the
ability to transact on their own, the difference between an accountable economy and a market
full of anonymous bots comes down to whether identity and reputation are verifiable on-chain.

That's the layer SAID is building. Capability is arriving fast. Trust has to arrive with it.
