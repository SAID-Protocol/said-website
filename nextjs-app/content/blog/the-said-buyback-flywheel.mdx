Most tokens launch first and figure out revenue later. SAID did it backwards.

1. Build the infrastructure.
2. Get real agents registered and transacting.
3. Turn on a 1% platform fee.
4. Use that revenue to buy back **$SAID**.

Revenue → buybacks → supply reduction → aligned incentives. Not a promise, not a
roadmap item — it's already running.

## Why backwards is the right order

The standard crypto playbook is to launch a token, generate hype, and "figure out
monetization post-TGE." More often than not, step one never arrives — the product
that was supposed to justify the token doesn't materialise, and the only thing
sustaining price is emissions dressed up as rewards.

SAID started from the other end. The agents came first: identities registered,
passports minted, transactions flowing through the network 24/7. Only once there
was real usage did the fee switch flip on. That ordering matters — it means the
token is backed by activity that already exists, not activity we hope to create.

No VC unlock cliffs. No team allocation dumps. No inflation games or token emissions
disguised as rewards. The supply pressure comes from one place: agents doing real
work, and the protocol using the fees from that work to buy back the token.

The math isn't complicated — it's just rare, because most projects skip step one:
